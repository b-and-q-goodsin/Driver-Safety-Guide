# Translation review brief

## For the reviewer
You are checking a safety notice for lorry drivers arriving at a UK distribution centre. The English is the management-approved text, so meaning must match it exactly. Open your language's sheet from the `translation-review` folder in Excel. Compare each draft column with the English master, fill in your name and language, mark Y or N, and write the corrected wording where needed.

Check in particular that:
- every instruction says what the English says, including small words: rule 2 says vehicles "should" take extra care (advice), rules 3 and 4 say "always", and rule 9 says the yellow fixed chocks are driven over in suitable vehicles
- rule 17 says site staff place the chocks and the driver checks they are removed
- a driver cannot read any rule as the opposite of what is meant
- words are what a professional driver in that language would use

To test on a phone, scan the reviewer QR card, or open the live address with `?review=1` on the end. Tap your language, read each page, then tap Read this page to listen.

If the text changes after sign-off, any recorded audio for that language must be recorded again.

## If your phone does not speak your language
Phones only speak languages whose voice is installed. Search Settings for "text-to-speech" on Android or "spoken content" on iPhone and download the voice for your language, then try again. If it still does not speak, tell the coordinator. That language will use a recording instead.

## Second check by Claude
Every rule and instruction in all 13 languages was read against the English. Google Translate could not be used from here, so this was my own reading, not a machine cross-check. It is not a substitute for a native reader, and it was weakest for Lithuanian and Latvian. Changes made to the reviewers' wording:
- Portuguese rule 13 and Bulgarian rule 13: removed "immediately", which is not in the approved English.
- Portuguese rule 1: "fechado" (fastened) instead of "apertado" (tight). Portuguese rule 8: plural verb to match "cavalo ou reboque".
- Latvian rules 13, 14 and 16: "vadības pārstāvis" (management representative) instead of "vadītājs", which also means "driver" and could be misread.
- Russian and Ukrainian acknowledgement boxes and Agree buttons: gender-neutral wording instead of forms like "прочитал(а)", which read badly aloud.
- Polish rule 2: clearer wording for "align the front of the vehicle with the red stop line".
Left as written by the reviewers: the firmer wording in rule 2 in several languages, and the strong word "under no circumstances" in some rule 10 translations.

## Differences from the gatehouse sheets (owner to be aware)
1. Language choice is the first screen, so a driver does not read English first.
2. The welcome note, the three acknowledgement boxes and the thank-you line are page wording, not from the sheets. They are needed for the page to work.
3. Goods-In item 1 says "while your vehicle is being tipped". The sheet reads "while your being tipped", which looks like a typing slip.
4. Goods-In item 3 reads "your hi-vis gives 360-degree visibility" for "your hi vis is 360 visibility".
5. Rule 12 names the subject (passengers, pets or children) in the sentence, because the sheet gives it only in the title.
6. Headings say "Goods-In" with a hyphen.
7. Goods-In item 6 now reads "when you are finished we will bring you your keys and paperwork". The sheet reads "when your finished with your keys and paperwork", which could be read two ways, and most reviewers read it as staff collecting the keys. This was agreed by the owner.
8. Rule 2: the English says extra-long vehicles "should" proceed with extra caution. Several translations use a firmer word ("must" or an instruction). This was accepted by the owner.
9. Bulgarian, Russian and Ukrainian write the speed as "miles per hour" instead of "mph". This was accepted by the owner.
